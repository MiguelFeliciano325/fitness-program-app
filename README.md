## 1. About Me (the User)

*Who am I in the context of this problem? What roles am I playing (student, employee, parent, athlete, gamer…)?*

I am a college student studying computer science while balancing school, part-time work, and personal commitments. I also play basketball and want to stay active, improve my strength, and feel more athletic.

My weekly schedule makes consistency difficult. I have classes on Mondays and Wednesdays that can run from 8 a.m. to 7:40 p.m., basketball on Tuesdays and  Wednesdays, and work at Subway from Friday through Sunday, usually 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. By the time I finish school or work, I can feel tired and have trouble following through on exercise plans.

I used to go to the gym consistently, but my routine fell apart after a trip to the Philippines and changes to my gym schedule. I know how to work out, but I struggle to make it a consistent part of my current life.

**The last 3 times this happened:**
*Real, recent moments. For each: when and where, what went wrong, what it cost, what you did.*

1. After a long school day: I get home tired, eat dinner around 8pm, Gym closes at either 11pm, 10pm, or 7pm. Instead of getting ready and going to the gym, I rest and tell myself that I will just go tomorrow. This causes me to miss a workout even when I still have time to exercise.
2. When I plan to go to the gym with my brother: I stayed home telling my brother I am too tired and instead went on my phone, since it is easier to skip going to the gym and exercising. Having my brother go with me helps me make plans, but does not stop me canceling it.
3. Skipping leg day: It ruins my streak and makes it difficult to maintain my routine, I use the missed session as a reason to take another day off, telling myself that it is supposed to be rest day today, which leads to more missed workout and makes it harder to stay consistent.

**What it costs me:**
*Be specific. How often, how much?*

- ⏱️ Time: Workout time is lost when I procrastinate, I spend time creating a routine that keeps breaking
- 💸 Money: Paying for gym access, I don't go consistently. $30/month
- 🔋 Energy: I feel sluggish, less athletic, restarting feels harder
- 🙂 Joy: The satisfaction of getting stronger, beating my PR's, seeing progress over time

## 2. Problem Statement

**Gut check:**

> I need **a way to follow through with my workout plans** because **I feel out of shape, heavier and procrastinate about working out**.

**Five Whys:**
1. Why do I miss workouts? I often feel tired after school or work, or get distracted with my phone and end up not going
2. Why does me being tired and distracted stop me from going to the gym? Resting and using my phone is easier than going to the gym and draining myself more
3. Why is it easy to skip going to the gym? Because my workout plans are not flexible, it does not account my changing schedule or how tired I am
4. Why do I lose momentum after missing gym sessions? I set a plan for the week like Push Pull Legs Rest but when I skip leg day the following day is a rest day and I end up prolonging my rest which ruins the momentum 
5. Why is maintaing a workout routine so difficult? I rely too much on motivation, fixed plans, instead of having a flexible routine that helps me stay accountable when I miss workouts

**Problem statement:**

> **When** I get home after a long day of school or work, especially after dinner around 8–8:30 PM or after a Subway shift around 3:30 PM,
> **I struggle to** follow through on my planned gym sessions or walks. I intend to exercise 4–5 times per week, but I usually only complete 1–2 sessions.
> **which costs me** opportunities to improve my strength, energy, and athletic ability, as well as the satisfaction of seeing my progress. I also spend about $30 per month on my gym membership but don't use it as consistently as I intend to.
> **This happens because** I make exercise plans based on how motivated I think I'll feel instead of considering how busy or tired I might be. After a long day, resting or using my phone feels easier and more rewarding than exercising. When I miss a planned session, I tell myself I'll go the next day, but I sometimes keep postponing or get distracted and lose track of my routine.
> **Right now I** I make workout plans, set personal goals, and sometimes plan sessions with my brother, **but** approaches don't always work because my brother may be busy, I may feel too tired or unmotivated, and I struggle to hold myself accountable when exercising on my own.

**I'll know this is solved when:** I consistently complete at least 4 intentional exercise sessions per week for 4 consecutive weeks. If I miss a planned session, I can make it up later in the same week instead of giving up on the rest of the week. My goal is also to become more independent with exercising and follow through without depending on my brother's availability. Gym workouts, home workouts, intentional exercise walks, and each basketball session count as one session.
